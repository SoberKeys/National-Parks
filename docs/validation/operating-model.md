## 1. What [PROJECT] is, and what it is not

[PROJECT] is a software product and a merchandise business.

**It is:**
- A mobile and web application that describes publicly-accessible hiking and running routes on open,
  established trails.
- A service that reviews a GPS track a user voluntarily submits and confirms whether it corresponds to
  a described route.
- A seller of physical commemorative merchandise — a printed card, a sticker, a pin, a patch, a
  passport book — shipped to a customer's home address.

**It is not:**
- An event organizer. We hold no events.
- A guide or outfitter. We accompany nobody and provide no equipment, instruction, transport, or
  supervision.
- A concessioner. We sell nothing on park land and operate no facility.
- A competition organizer. We publish no ranked results.

## 2. Where commercial activity occurs

**Entirely off National Park Service land.**

Every transaction is an online purchase completed on our website, before or after any park visit,
processed by a third-party payment processor, and fulfilled by post to a residential address. No money
changes hands in a park. No product is delivered in a park. Nothing is sold, marketed, or transacted on
NPS-managed land.

## 3. What happens inside the park

**An individual recreates independently on open, publicly-accessible trails, exactly as they would
without us.**

They arrive by their own means, pay any entrance fee directly to NPS, park in public parking, and walk
or run on an open trail during normal visiting hours. They carry a phone or a watch, as very large
numbers of visitors already do. They are subject to every park rule that applies to any other visitor,
and are individually responsible for compliance.

There is no way to distinguish a [PROJECT] user in a park from any other visitor. There is no
gathering, no group, no marker, no timing device, and no scheduled moment.

## 4. What we have on park land

**Nothing.**

No staff. No guides. No volunteers. No equipment. No signage, markers, flags, or course marking. No
vehicles. No tents, tables, or stations. No aid, water, or supplies. No photographers. No timing
apparatus. No merchandise. No gatherings. No scheduled start times. No simultaneous participation.

## 5. How routes are selected

- Only **open, established, publicly-accessible trails** already promoted for public use by the park.
- **Never** off-trail or cross-country.
- **Never** through a permit-gated feature. Angels Landing, Old Rag and Half Dome are explicitly and
  permanently excluded, along with any comparable feature.
- **No user-generated routes.** Every route is curated, reviewed and published by us, which means every
  route is something we can take responsibility for and remove.
- Wilderness status, group-size limits, seasonal closures and use restrictions are checked before
  publication. `[TO CONFIRM — per-park review is on the validation checklist]`

## 6. Closures, permits, reservations and alerts

We surface official requirements; we never route around them.

- Park alerts are pulled from the **NPS Data API** and displayed with their source and the time they
  were retrieved.
- Timed-entry reservations, entrance fees and feature-specific permits are shown prominently, with a
  link to the official booking channel.
- Every route carries an explicit statement that the user is responsible for permits, closures,
  reservations and current conditions.
- **Any route can be unpublished instantly** by an administrator. A closure alert, a park request, or
  our own judgment removes it within minutes.

## 7. Why there are no public timed leaderboards

A deliberate, permanent design decision, made for two independent reasons:

1. **Safety.** Rewarding speed in terrain with heat, altitude, exposure and limited daylight would
   encourage exactly the behaviour that injures people. We will not do it.
2. **Character of the activity.** We do not want the activity to be, or to resemble, a competitive
   event.

Elapsed time is shown to the individual who recorded it, and on artifacts they choose to share about
their own activity. It is never ranked against other users publicly.

Recorded as [ADR-0005](../adr/0005-no-events-no-leaderboards-regulatory-posture.md).

## 8. Non-affiliation and intellectual property

- [PROJECT] is **not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by** the National Park Service. This is
  stated explicitly on public surfaces.
- **We do not use the NPS Arrowhead** or any NPS insignia, in any form, anywhere.
- We use no NPS imagery, uniforms, or trade dress.
- Our physical passport product is independently designed with independent trade dress and is not
  connected to the Passport To Your National Parks® program. `[TO CONFIRM — counsel question 6]`
- Use of park names is descriptive only. `[TO CONFIRM — counsel question 2]`

## 9. Scale and intent of the current activity

The current phase is a **45-day research pilot**: approximately **25–35 individuals** across **three
parks** (Acadia, Shenandoah, Zion), each completing a single route on their own schedule, at a time of
their own choosing, alone or with whoever they were already travelling with.

The purpose is to learn whether the concept is worth building at all. We are writing before scaling
rather than after, because we would rather understand the National Park Service's position early than
present it with a fait accompli.

## 10. What we are asking

Whether, on this description, the National Park Service considers this activity to require a Commercial
Use Authorization or any other authorization.

**What we are not asking for:** no event permit, no special use permit, no on-park presence, no
endorsement, no use of NPS marks, no exclusivity, and no exception to any rule.

## 11. Voluntary commitments

Regardless of the answer, we commit to:
- No organized events and no on-park presence.
- No public timed leaderboards.
- Removing any route from any park on request, within one business day.
- Surfacing official rules, closures and reservation requirements rather than routing around them.
- Not using NPS marks or implying endorsement.
- Naming a single point of contact for any park that wants one.
