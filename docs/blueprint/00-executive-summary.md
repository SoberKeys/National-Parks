## A. Executive summary

[PROJECT] is a mobile platform that converts physical effort in extraordinary places into verified,
collectible achievements.

A user opens a map of the United States. Sixty-three National Parks sit on it, locked. Each park holds
a small set of curated, legal, publicly-accessible routes — a 5K, a 10K, a longer trail challenge. The
user travels there on their own, runs or hikes the route, and the app records GPS. The platform
verifies the track against the route and unlocks the park. The user gets a digital achievement, a
shareable card, a position in their own 63-park journey, and — if they want it — a physical object in
the mail that proves it.

The business is not a race company. We organize nothing on the ground. We sell software and objects;
the customer supplies the trip.

**What is genuinely new.** Existing park apps let you *say* you were there — Chimani, ParkPassport,
Park'd and the official NPS app all offer check-ins or badges (`FACT`). Existing fitness apps let you
*record* that you ran. Nobody sells the combination: a **verified physical accomplishment tied to a
specific, prestigious geography, rendered as a collectible with a finite, completable set.**

Finiteness is the asset. 63 is a number a human can hold in their head and want to finish. "63/63" is
a status object that does not currently exist.

**The honest risk, stated up front.** This is the longest cycle-time loop in consumer fitness. Strava's
loop is daily. The Conqueror's loop is daily. Ours is seasonal to annual. That is the thing most likely
to kill the company, and the product loop and MVP scope are built around it.

## B. Core value proposition

> *Why would someone use this instead of just tracking a run on Strava?*

Strava answers "how did I do today?" We answer **"what have I done with my life, and what's next?"**

Five things Strava structurally cannot give this user:

1. **A finite, completable set.** Strava has infinite miles and no end state. We have 63 and a finish
   line. Collections need edges.
2. **Verification that means something.** On Strava an activity is a log. Here an activity is a
   *claim*, adjudicated against a defined route and either accepted or not. Scarcity requires a gate.
3. **Geographic prestige.** A 10K in your neighbourhood and a 10K in Grand Teton are identical to
   Strava. They are not identical to a human being.
4. **A physical artifact.** Strava gives you a heart-rate graph. We give you an object on a shelf that
   a friend picks up and asks about.
5. **A reason to book a flight.** No fitness app has ever generated travel intent. This one is built on
   it, and that is also where the long-term revenue lives.

`RECOMMENDATION` — internal positioning, not a tagline and not branding:
*"The permanent record of what you accomplished, not what you did today."*

**Counter-argument worth hearing:** Strava could ship "National Park badges" in a quarter. They would
be free, digital, unverified against a route, and impossible to make prestigious inside an
infinite-content app. It would hurt our top of funnel and not touch our paying customer, who is buying
an object and a status. Survivable — but plan for it.
